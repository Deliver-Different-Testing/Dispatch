# Job Splitting — Split Screen & Price Breakdown UI

**Author:** George
**For:** Jacob
**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Status:** Draft — screenshots pending
**Related work:** Jacob's fix this week that locks the split amount so it no longer
changes after a job has been split, on the parent (customer charging) job.

---

## 1. Why this change

Jacob's fix stabilised the numbers. The remaining problem is that the operator
can no longer *read* them, and can't act on them without leaving the screen.

After a split, the parent job's price breakdown becomes a flat list of every line
item generated by the split screen. From that list the dispatcher cannot tell:

- which leg a line belongs to,
- which driver is running that leg,
- what that driver is actually being paid for it,
- how to change a driver's pay for one leg without disturbing the customer charge.

The split screen itself is also easy to misread: the percentage the operator edits
there is a *revenue* allocation, not driver pay, but nothing on the screen says so.

---

## 2. The three stages today

> Screenshots to be dropped into `mockups/job-splitting/` and linked here.

### Stage 1 — Pre-split price breakdown

![Pre-split price breakdown](../mockups/job-splitting/01-pre-split-price-breakdown.png)

The original job before the dispatcher splits it.

- **Revenue** column — the charge to the customer for each component of the job.
- **Cost** column — the courier payment.

*(Ignore the negative fuel line: that's a rating quirk in staging, not in scope here.)*

### Stage 2 — Split screen

![Split screen](../mockups/job-splitting/02-split-screen.png)

The dispatcher divides the job into legs. Dispatch seeds each leg with a share of
the **charge** based on that leg's mileage, and the operator can edit the **%** per
leg to change the allocation.

Two things are true here that the screen does not communicate:

1. The % being edited allocates **customer revenue** across the legs.
2. It is **not** what the driver is paid. Driver pay is derived proportionately from
   that leg's share, according to how the original rate is configured for customer
   charge vs. driver pay.

### Stage 3 — Parent price breakdown after splitting

![Parent price breakdown after split](../mockups/job-splitting/03-parent-price-breakdown.png)

The parent job's breakdown now lists every item produced by the split screen, flat
and unattributed. This is the screen that is confusing in practice:

- No indication of which line belongs to which leg.
- No indication of which driver is doing which leg.
- No route or sequence context (collection → delivery per leg).
- No way to adjust what a driver is paid for their leg from here.

---

## 3. What we want instead

### 3.1 Parent price breakdown becomes leg-grouped

Replace the flat list with one collapsible group per leg. Collapsed, each leg shows
a single summary row; expanded, it shows the underlying rate lines that make it up.

Proposed summary row per leg:

| Leg | Route | Driver | Share % | Revenue | Driver pay | Margin |
|-----|-------|--------|---------|---------|-----------|--------|
| 1 of 3 | Auckland CBD → Manukau depot | *driver name* | 34% | $x | $y | $z |
| 2 of 3 | Manukau depot → Hamilton depot | *driver name* | 41% | $x | $y | $z |
| 3 of 3 | Hamilton depot → consignee | *driver name* | 25% | $x | $y | $z |
| **Total** | | | **100%** | **= pre-split revenue** | **$Y** | **$Z** |

Requirements:

- The leg **total revenue must equal the pre-split job revenue** and must not drift —
  this is the invariant Jacob's fix established, and the UI should make it visible
  rather than just enforce it silently. Show the total row permanently.
- Driver name (and vehicle/fleet number if available) on every leg row. If a leg is
  unassigned, say **Unassigned** explicitly rather than leaving it blank.
- Expanding a leg shows that leg's rate lines in the existing Revenue / Cost column
  format, so nothing is lost for operators who need the detail.
- Keep a **Job totals** section at the bottom matching the pre-split layout, so the
  parent job can still be reconciled at a glance against what the customer is charged.

### 3.2 Driver pay is editable per leg, from the breakdown

The dispatcher should be able to change what a driver is paid for their leg without
leaving the price breakdown and without touching the customer charge.

- Driver pay per leg is **derived** by default (from the rate's charge/pay
  relationship applied to that leg's share). Show it as a derived value.
- Allow an **override** on a leg's driver pay. An overridden leg is visually flagged
  and shows the derived value it replaced, so it's obvious a human changed it.
- Editing driver pay **must not** change customer revenue for any leg.
- Editing a leg's revenue share **must not** silently change an overridden driver pay;
  it re-derives pay only for legs that are not overridden.

### 3.3 Split screen gets a driver-pay preview

Add a read-only **Driver pay** column beside the charge allocation on the split screen,
plus a one-line explainer:

> The % allocates the **customer charge** across legs. Driver pay is derived from each
> leg's share using the job's rate setup.

This kills the main misreading at the point it happens, and means the dispatcher sees
the pay consequence of a % edit before committing the split.

---

## 4. Calculation rules to preserve

1. **Customer charge total is fixed at split time** and does not change when legs are
   edited afterwards (Jacob's fix). Editing a leg's % redistributes within that fixed
   total; it never grows or shrinks it.
2. **Percentages always sum to 100.** Define and implement the rounding remainder rule
   — recommend allocating the remainder to the largest leg — so the leg rows always
   add to the parent total to the cent.
3. **Driver pay is derived from the leg share**, using the charge-to-pay relationship
   already configured on the original rate. Splitting must not change the pay basis.
4. **Overrides are sticky.** Once a leg's driver pay is overridden it survives
   subsequent re-allocations until explicitly reset.

---

## 5. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Parent price breakdown after a split is grouped by leg, one summary row per leg,
      expandable to the underlying rate lines.
- [ ] Every leg row shows the assigned driver, or **Unassigned**.
- [ ] A permanent totals row shows leg revenue summing exactly to the pre-split revenue.
- [ ] Driver pay per leg can be overridden from the parent breakdown.
- [ ] Overriding driver pay leaves all customer revenue figures unchanged.
- [ ] Changing a leg's % leaves overridden driver pay untouched and re-derives the rest.
- [ ] Percentages and money always reconcile to 100% / the parent total, with the
      rounding remainder rule applied consistently.
- [ ] Split screen shows a derived driver-pay figure per leg before the split is saved.

## 6. Open questions for Jacob

1. Is the charge-to-pay relationship on a rate always expressible as a proportion of
   the leg's charge, or are there rate types (flat pay, per-km pay, waiting time)
   where pay can't be derived from the leg's revenue share at all?
2. When a leg is reassigned to a different driver after the split, should derived pay
   re-calculate against the new driver's pay setup, or stay as allocated?
3. Are driver-pay overrides already supported anywhere in the data model, or is that
   new storage?
4. Can a split be edited after any leg has been PODed / invoiced, and if so what should
   be locked at that point?
5. Does the parent job need an audit trail of split edits (who changed what %, when)?

---

## 7. Out of scope

- The negative fuel line in staging (rating quirk, tracked separately).
- Any change to how the initial mileage-based allocation is calculated.
- Invoicing and driver-payment-run behaviour downstream of the split.
