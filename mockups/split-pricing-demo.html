What this mockup is testing

The proposed replacement for the parent job’s Price Breakdown once a job has been split. Figures are the real staging job KT4070V / KT4071V (US$114.00, 80/20 by road distance), so every number here should match the screenshots. The negative fuel cost is the known staging rating quirk and is carried through deliberately.

  1. The parent keeps its own five rows. No Part A / Part B expansion — cost is broken out into a column per leg instead. Spec §3
  2. Reconciliation is visible. The leg cost column totals are exactly each child job’s Total Cost; the Revenue total is exactly the headline price. Nothing can drift. §3
  3. Edit a Revenue cell → every leg’s revenue re-derives by share and the header updates. Edit a leg cost cell → no revenue figure moves anywhere. Zero is valid, so a driver can be left unpaid for an item. §4, §6.1
  4. Overridden costs are flagged amber, show the derived value they replaced, and can be reset. They survive share changes. §4.4
  5. Per-item shares. Click the share pill on any row to give a single charge its own split — for a charge only one leg incurred. §3
  6. Locking. Tick Invoiced to lock the Revenue column; tick Leg A settled to lock just that leg’s cost column while the others stay live. Delivery locks nothing. §6.5
  7. 3 and 4 legs. The modal widens first; only then do the leg columns scroll, with Item and Revenue frozen. §6.2
  8. One component, two modes. Switch to Confirm split for the pre-split view — same grid, legs labelled by mileage rather than job number, and a Confirm & Split action. §7
  9. Rounding. Remainders go to the largest leg, so leg amounts always re-sum to the parent to the cent. Try 3 legs and set a revenue of 10.00 to see it. §4.6
